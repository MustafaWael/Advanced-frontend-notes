# STAR Story Bank

## Maturity Target

- Priority: #important
- Study time: 2-3 hours once, then 10 minutes before each interview
- Interview signal: any behavioral prompt maps to a prepared story within seconds — told in ~2 minutes, with numbers, ending on the result and lesson.
- Production signal: none — this is pure interview infrastructure. But mining stories forces you to notice what you actually did this year.
- Fast track: fill the two must-have rows (disagreement, ownership) tonight; one story per week after.

## How This Bank Works

Six to eight stories cover essentially every behavioral question, because interviewers rotate the same competencies with different wording. Prepare stories, not answers: each story below gets tagged with every competency it can serve, and in the interview you re-angle the *same* story to the question asked.

Format per story — STAR with the vault's additions:

- **S**ituation: one sentence of context. Interviewers don't need your org chart.
- **T**ask: what *you* specifically were responsible for.
- **A**ction: the meat — decisions, not activities ("I chose X over Y because…" beats "I worked hard on…").
- **R**esult: a number if humanly possible (latency, error rate, days saved, tickets killed). Estimated is fine; say it's estimated.
- **+Lesson**: what you'd do differently. This line is what makes a good story great.
- **+Mechanism**: the technical detail you can go deep on if pressed — behavioral answers from frontend engineers earn a follow-up like "wait, how did you fix that race condition?" and the story must survive it.

> [!tip] Rehearse out loud, timed: 2 minutes per story, then a 30-second compressed cut of each. Interviewers who want more will ask; running long on story one steals time from your strongest material. Re-read this bank the morning of every interview — recall speed, not content, is what preparation buys.

> [!warning] Never fabricate and never composite two events into one — follow-up questions ("what did your tech lead say then?") shred invented details, and the stress tell is visible. A modest true story with a real number beats an epic vague one every time.
