# 05 - Common Mistakes to Avoid

System design interviews are tricky because they are open-ended and hard to practice for. Here are the most common pitfalls candidates fall into and how to avoid them.

### 1. Jumping into Answering Immediately
- **The Mistake:** Hearing the prompt and immediately drawing boxes or writing code.
- **The Consequence:** You end up answering the *wrong* question perfectly. System design prompts are deliberately under-specified. If you don't clarify the scope, you'll design a product the interviewer didn't have in mind.
- **The Fix:** Start by gathering requirements and clarifying assumptions. Ask questions!

### 2. Approaching the Question in an Unstructured Manner
- **The Mistake:** Talking about whatever comes to mind without a clear progression.
- **The Consequence:** The interview feels messy and you miss critical evaluation areas.
- **The Fix:** Use the **RADIO framework**. Write down "RADIO" on the whiteboard at the start and check off the letters as you go to ensure you've covered everything.

### 3. Insisting on Only One Solution
- **The Mistake:** Refusing to consider alternative approaches, or believing there is only one "right" way.
- **The Consequence:** You fail the "Exploration and Tradeoffs" evaluation axis.
- **The Fix:** Acknowledge that there are multiple ways to solve a problem. State the alternatives (even the bad ones), explain *why* they are bad or suboptimal, and justify the tradeoff you're making.

### 4. Remaining Silent
- **The Mistake:** Thinking internally for long stretches of time.
- **The Consequence:** The interviewer cannot evaluate your thought process.
- **The Fix:** Think out loud! Treat the interviewer like a coworker. Bounce ideas off them, discuss issues you've identified, and collaborate.

### 5. Going Down a Rabbit Hole
- **The Mistake:** Diving way too deep into a specific component before defining the high-level architecture.
- **The Consequence:** You run out of time and fail to provide a complete system design.
- **The Fix:** Always define the high-level design first. If you want to dive deep, ask the interviewer: *"I can dive deeper into how this specific component works if you'd like, or we can move on to the next part?"*

### 6. Using Buzzwords You Can't Explain
- **The Mistake:** Throwing around terms like "Virtual DOM", "Partial Hydration", or "Streaming SSR" to sound smart.
- **The Consequence:** The interviewer will probe you on these terms. If you can't explain them, it's a huge red flag indicating surface-level knowledge.
- **The Fix:** Never drop a term you can't defend under follow-up questioning. If you don't know it deeply, don't say it.
