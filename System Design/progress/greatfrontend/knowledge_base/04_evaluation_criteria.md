# 04 - Evaluation Criteria for Front End System Design

During the interview, interviewers score the signals and behaviors you display. The more detailed and mature your answers, the higher the leveling recommendation. 

> [!WARNING]
> **Interviewers score spoken signals, not unspoken reasoning.** If you thought about a tradeoff but didn't verbalize it, it didn't happen. Make every decision visible through narration and diagrams!

## Key Evaluation Axes

### 1. Problem Exploration (R)
- Ask relevant clarifying questions to minimize ambiguity.
- Gather both functional and non-functional requirements.
- Define the scope of the problem.
- Identify the most important aspects of the product to focus on.

### 2. Architecture (A, D, I)
- Break down the problem into smaller, independent parts.
- Define clear responsibilities for each component.
- Articulate how components work together (API).
- Ensure the architecture is practical, scalable, and reusable.

### 3. Technical Proficiency (A, O)
- Demonstrate deep knowledge of front end fundamentals, technologies, and APIs.
- Dive into domain areas relevant to the problem (e.g., Performance, Networking, Accessibility, HTML/CSS).
- Identify tricky areas and propose solutions with their tradeoffs.

### 4. Exploration and Tradeoffs (A, D, I, O)
- Propose multiple possible solutions for a given sub-problem.
- Clearly explain the pros and cons of each approach.
- Recommend the best solution for the specific context of the question.
- **Pro Tip:** Show the alternatives you rejected. Mentioning 2-3 options and articulating why the chosen one fits is a much stronger signal than arriving at the same final answer silently.

### 5. Product and UX Sense (O)
- Consider the user experience: loading states, perceived performance, mobile-friendliness, keyboard navigation.
- Anticipate error cases (network failures, bad inputs) and suggest ways to handle them gracefully.

### 6. Communication and Collaboration (All)
- Convey thoughts clearly and concisely.
- Explain complex concepts simply.
- Engage the interviewer: ask for their opinion, be open to feedback, and incorporate their suggestions into your design.
